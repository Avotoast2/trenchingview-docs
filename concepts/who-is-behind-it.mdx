---
title: "Who is behind it"
description: "How the team line is built: interactions nominate, claims decide, previous employers are excluded, and the official account is inferred when nothing lists it."
---

Projects rarely name their team. The site says nothing, the official bio says nothing, and yet the founder is right there — quoted by the official account, followed by it, with "building @project" in their own bio. The team line is built to find that person and **only** that person.

## Interactions nominate, claims decide

Behaviour finds candidates: the official account quotes, reposts, replies to and follows people; third parties write "built by @x"; a model reading X names handles; the followers of a silent official account are ranked by how many notable accounts follow them. All of that decides **whose bio and posts get read**. None of it puts anyone on the team line.

An account joins the team only with **its own claim**:

| Claim | Example |
|---|---|
| **Bio, present tense** | `building @listedexchange` · `co-founder @umia_finance` · `dev @insertculthere` · `Making Capital Formation work for Ventures at @umia_finance` |
| **Posts in the author register** | A meaningful share of recent posts about the project, in the first person: "we shipped", "how we can tweak the LPs", "excited to announce @x" |
| **Hard evidence** | A GitHub contributor of the linked repo; named as the dev by two or more third parties |

## Reading the bio: relation words decide

The phrase around each mention says what the account *is* to the project:

| Phrase | Read as | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| building · founder · co-founder · CEO · CTO · dev · lead · core · team · working on | **team**, with the role word | joins the team line as `builder`, `co-founder`, `dev`… |
| prev. · previously · ex- · formerly · former · alumni | **previous employer** | never team; shown as chip meta `prev. @PolynomialFi` |
| investor · backed by · angel · advisor | **backer** | not team |
| fan of · holding · bullish | **holder** | dropped |

A handle that only appears as *someone else's* previous employer or backer is an organisation mention. It is never even a candidate.

<Note>
The case that set this rule: a founder whose bio read `building @listedexchange | prev. @PolynomialFi`. The earlier version listed all three handles as team. Now: `@Akhileyyy · builder · prev. @PolynomialFi`, the project's account on its own row, and PolynomialFi nowhere.
</Note>

## The contract address in a bio

The strongest claim an account can make is to put the token's **contract address** in its bio. The bio parser treats the address like the project's name: `building ZZZ | CA 0x7dbf…` is a team claim, a bare `ZZZ 0x7dbf…` is a plain claim, and `supporter of ZZZ (0x7dbf…)` is a **supporter** — a fan account that pins the contract, listed under *possibly* on the official-X row, never made official and never on the team line.

The address cuts the other way too. An account met under this token's name whose bio pins a **different** contract raises a **contract mismatch** finding: one of the two tokens is an impostor, and the dossier will not pick a side for you. Pair it with the lookalike finding on the Liquidity panel, which names the older, deeper token this one is named after.

## How sure the dossier is: tiers

Every name on the Project panel carries a tier chip, and hovering it shows the evidence behind the name.

| Tier | Means |
|---|---|
| **proven** | Two independent sources tie the account to the contract — for example the listing names it and the verified contract source names it, or the site links the account and the account's bio links the site |
| **claimed** | One source ties it to the contract: the listing, the creator's launch metadata, or the account's own bio carrying the address |

For people rather than the official account: a personal X profile the project's own site links, under any heading, is **declared** by the project and appears on the creator row as "linked from the site". An account that only quoted, reposted or posted about the token appears under **shilled by** with what it did. A candidate whose own posts from before the launch describe the project's idea gets the note "wrote the thesis N days before launch", a hint next to the name, never a membership on its own.
| **inferred** | Something points at the account (a site link, a name match, a shared deployer) but nothing ties it to the contract yet |
| **unknown** | Nothing above the bar. Posting the contract, even first, is not enough on its own — snipers and call channels do that within seconds of a pool |

Two accounts that cannot be separated become a **conflict** finding rather than a coin flip. Team members carry their own tier: **declared** by the project (the official bio, the site's team section, a handle the creator wrote into the contract), **claimed** by the account itself (its bio or its posts), or **inferred** from hard evidence (a GitHub contributor, named as dev by several third parties).

## Accounts that only interact

The official quoting, reposting or following someone means the project likes them. It does not make them the team. Those accounts appear as a muted **around it** line in the Social section — the signal is kept, without masquerading as membership.

## The official account

The project's own account is resolved from **typed evidence anchored on the contract address**, never from the first link that looks right. Seven channels contribute, cheapest first:

| Channel | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Listing | The pair's socials and website, each typed first: an X profile is a candidate, an X post is the launch post (its author a candidate), an X page or a link-page host is neither |
| Launchpad | What the creator typed at launch — the token's metadata on Solana, the verified contract source on EVM chains (the Pons template bakes X, Telegram and website into the contract) |
| Chain | The real deployer (through launcher contracts) and its other tokens the desk already resolved |
| Ledger | Everything the desk has seen about this contract before: earlier reads, every post carrying the address from the X intake and the FOMO feed, listings |
| Creator (Solana) | The creator wallet as a source: its pump.fun profile and the X it links, the launchpad's own ledger of that creator's other launches, and the wallet that first funded it. Two fresh creator wallets funded by the same wallet are treated as the same people in **other launches** |
| Telegram | The project's Telegram from its public page: group or channel, how many members, whether the description or a recent channel post carries the contract, and which X account or site it links. A personal contact card is not a community |
| Site | The site itself: does it carry the contract, what does it link |
| X | Who posts the contract and who posted it first, and the bios of the few candidates that matter |
| GitHub | Does the README carry the contract or link the site |

Each slot resolves to a winner with a tier (see above) and its alternatives. A tie between two supported accounts is reported as a conflict. Posting the contract first is medium evidence on its own — snipers do that too — and becomes decisive only next to a second source. Infrastructure accounts (chains, DEXes, launchpads) are never a project's official, unless the token is that project's own. When nothing reaches the bar the row reads **unknown** with the alternatives, and the downstream checks that need an official (X history, notable followers) do not run on a guess.

## Promoters

Everyone else who pushed the token — trusted voices, notable accounts from the read of X — with a link to the post where they said it. The official account, the team, anyone a model named as behind the project, and infrastructure accounts (chains, DEXes, tools, whale-alert bots) are excluded by rule.

## When X is down

If the X provider is unavailable, the team check cannot run. The line then shows the accounts a model named as behind the project, marked **named on X · unverified**, rather than nothing — and the dossier carries a PARTIAL banner. A Fresh run once the provider is back replaces them with verified chips.
